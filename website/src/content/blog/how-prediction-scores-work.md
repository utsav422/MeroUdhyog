Here's something most wholesalers know but never write down: their regular customers don't order randomly. They order on a rhythm. Sagar Traders buys every 18 days or so. Everest Hardware is more erratic, maybe every 40 days. Boudha Suppliers orders in bursts around festivals.

If you can see that rhythm, you can act on it. That's all a prediction score is.

## What the score actually measures

There's no magic here, and we're the first to say it. For every customer and every product, we look at the gaps between their past orders and ask three questions:

- **Recency** — how long since they last bought? (Weighted 40%)
- **Frequency** — how often do they usually buy? (Weighted 30%)
- **Consistency** — how steady is that rhythm? (Weighted 30%)

Combine those and you get an interest score from 0 to 100, plus a predicted next order date. A customer who buys like clockwork lands in the nineties. Someone with two scattered orders years apart stays low — and we tell you there isn't enough data rather than pretending.

## Turning a score into a phone call

A score is only useful if it changes what you do at 9am. So each customer-product pair gets one plain recommendation:

| Status | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On track | Plenty of time until their usual reorder | Leave them alone |
| Due soon | Their window is opening | Send a message |
| Overdue | They've passed their usual gap | Call them personally |
| Needs data | Not enough history yet | Just keep selling |

That's the whole system. No black box, no "the AI decided." If a customer is marked overdue, you can look at their actual order history and see exactly why.

## Why transparency matters more than accuracy

An opaque forecast makes you nervous — you don't know whether to trust it. A transparent one makes you *smarter*. When you call an overdue customer and they say "actually, yes, I was about to order," you learn your own business's rhythm. When they say "we've switched suppliers," you learn something more valuable.

Over time, the scores teach you things about your customer base you never had written down. That's worth more than a percentage.

## Where the score is strongest

Predictions are sharpest for customers with regular, repeat business — exactly the relationships you'd otherwise take for granted and lose quietly. For one-off buyers, they'll honestly tell you there's not enough data. That honesty is the point: we'd rather show you nothing than a confident wrong answer.

Start by entering your orders consistently for a month. The scores fill themselves in from there — and one morning you'll open the dashboard and find a name you'd been meaning to call anyway, flagged overdue. That's the day it starts paying back.
