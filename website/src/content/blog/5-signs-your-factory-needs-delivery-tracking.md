Deliveries are where a well-run factory and a chaotic one look identical from the inside — until something goes wrong. Here are the five signals that your delivery process has outgrown phone calls and memory.

## 1. "Where's the van?" is a daily question

When the same question gets asked every single morning, it means nobody — including you — actually knows the answer without making a call. That call is a cost. Multiply it by every customer who phones in.

## 2. Customers tell you about late deliveries before your own team does

If a customer has to complain to find out where their order is, your delivery visibility ends at your own gate. Good tracking means *you* know a delivery is running late before the customer does — and you can call them first.

## 3. You can't tell which route is worth it

If you're running delivery vans on multiple routes and you can't say which route costs the most time or fuel per order, you're guessing. Routes with lots of stops that never convert into repeat orders are quietly expensive.

## 4. Reassigning an agent is chaos

When one delivery agent is sick, does the day's plan survive? If reassigning an agent means someone has to reconstruct the whole day from memory, you're one absence away from a bad day.

## 5. Proof of delivery lives in someone's head

Without a recorded time, location or signature at the drop-off point, every "I never got it" becomes a he-said-she-said. Proof of delivery protects your business and your delivery staff equally.

## What good looks like

A proper delivery system — the kind built into Mero Udhyog — gives you three things: a live map of where every agent is, a route plan that survives absences, and a timestamped record of every drop. None of it requires new hardware. Your delivery team's phones are enough.

If three or more of the signs above sound familiar, the fix isn't a better notebook. It's a system that sees the whole route at once.
