Every factory owner we talk to wants the same thing: to stop losing track of money without turning their business upside down to do it. The mistake is trying to digitise everything in one go. You don't need to. You need to pick the right first thing.

## Start with what you already sell

The temptation is to start with accounting. Don't. Accounting is the *output* of operations, and if your operational data is messy, your books will be messy too.

Start with your **products**. Write down what you sell, in the units you sell it in — sacks, cartons, pieces, kilos. If a product has variations (size, weight, colour), keep them under one parent product so you're not maintaining ten near-identical entries.

This one step immediately gives you two things: a consistent name for everything, and a place for stock to begin counting itself.

## Then record the orders you'd record anyway

You already write orders down somewhere — a book, a pad, a WhatsApp message. Move that habit into one place. The point isn't to change how you sell. The point is to make every order update the stock automatically, so your inventory stops being a guess.

- One order entry per customer
- One line per product, with quantity and price
- Payment status next to the order (paid, partial, unpaid)

That's it. Within a week your stock numbers reflect reality, and you'll start noticing items that silently ran out.

## Put the khata in last — but do put it in

Credit is the emotional core of most Nepali businesses, and it's where disputes live. Once your orders are digital, the khata becomes simple: a payment from a customer gets applied to their oldest unpaid order first. No more guessing which bill someone was settling.

> The rule of thumb: products → orders → payments. Each step makes the next one easier.

## What to *not* do first

Don't start with reports, dashboards or forecasting. They only make sense once the underlying data exists. A forecast built on two weeks of half-entered orders will be wrong, and a wrong forecast erodes trust faster than no forecast at all.

Give it a month of clean order entry, and *then* the predictions start earning their place.

## The one-week test

If you can, in one week: (1) list every product you sell, (2) enter every order as it happens, and (3) mark payments against customers — you'll already be ahead of most businesses. Everything else in Mero Udhyog builds on that foundation.

Start where it's easiest to be consistent. Consistency beats completeness every time.
